FAQ

Answers to common questions: courses for adults and kids, IELTS.

How many levels do we teach?

Our General English programme has five levels: Beginner, Elementary, Pre-Intermediate, Intermediate and Upper-Intermediate. Each level is carefully built on the skills gained at the previous stage, so progress in the language is smooth. After finishing Upper-Intermediate, students can move on to our specialised IELTS preparation course, which helps them reach the score they want in the IELTS exam.

Do we have a course for kids?

Yes. The kids course has four levels:

At Level 4 there is another option: a child can start the General English course at Beginner level.

After completing all 4 levels, a child reaches the “start of Elementary” level by adult standards.

Important:

The kids programme lasts 8–12 months, because:

  • the learning material is simplified for the child’s age,
  • children’s cognitive development is taken into account,
  • the workload is spread out more gently than for adults.

What an adult learns in 3 months, a child learns in about 12.

How long does the course take?

Each level of the General English course at Sevenine takes about three months. That is around 36 lessons of 90 minutes each, three times a week. This structure makes learning effective and helps students make real progress in English.

What is the General English course?

Our General English course develops all four language skills: listening, reading, writing and speaking. At the same time, we work on improving grammar. The course is based on Navigate, a programme from Oxford University Press that is known and recognised around the world as an effective way to learn and teach English. We use this coursebook as the base for teaching General English. On top of that, from Elementary level we use an additional programme of our own to develop speaking quickly. Teachers also add extra materials from different sources.

What is the IELTS course?

Our IELTS course focuses on the four main skills — speaking, writing, listening and reading — without a special focus on grammar. Students don’t study grammar topics; instead, they polish their language skills at an advanced level to prepare for the IELTS exam. The course also teaches techniques for doing well in the exam. It lasts about three to four months. To join, a student needs to have finished Upper-Intermediate. However, if a student is happy with a lower IELTS score, such as 5 or 6, they can start the course after finishing Intermediate. We usually recommend finishing Upper-Intermediate first, but in exceptional cases, when the exam date is close, a student can move to the IELTS course straight after Intermediate.

What makes us different?

First, we give every student individual attention. Unlike other learning centres, where classes can have 14 students or more, at Sevenine groups are limited to 8 people — at almost the same price students pay at other popular centres with groups of 14.

Second, a high share of our students get at least 7 in IELTS — you can see this for yourself by scrolling through our Instagram.

Third, our teachers are our treasure. And we don’t just mean their high IELTS scores. We choose every teacher for great presentation skills and charisma, which is why our lessons are fun and interesting!

Fourth, we offer bonuses such as Speaking Workshops for extra speaking practice and the Sevenine Shop for motivation.

What programme are the lessons based on?

General English lessons follow the Oxford Navigate programme. Oxford Navigate is a complete English course for adult learners. It combines practical language skills with real-life situations and covers a wide range of levels. The programme takes a balanced approach, combining grammar, vocabulary and pronunciation with reading, writing, listening and speaking skills. The content is organised around modern topics, which makes learning relevant and engaging. Oxford Navigate also includes digital resources and interactive elements that support both classroom and self-study. This modern approach gives students not only a theoretical understanding of English but also the confidence to use it in everyday situations. We use this coursebook as the base for teaching General English. On top of that, from Elementary level we use an additional programme of our own to develop speaking quickly. Teachers also add extra materials from different sources.

IELTS lessons follow our own programme, which focuses on improving all four skills: reading, writing, listening and speaking. Teachers use handouts and online files.

Do we have academic support?

We don’t offer a separate academic support service, because we believe learning directly with the main teachers is more effective. In any school, the main teacher usually has more experience and knowledge than academic support staff. Our classes are small — up to 8 people — so our main teachers can give every student individual attention and answer questions right in the lesson, which makes learning deeper and better. That’s why our students don’t need academic support.

Also, our students can study at our centre outside lesson time, and if they have questions about a topic, they can ask any teacher who is free for help.

Can a student’s balance be frozen?

No, we don’t freeze student balances.

Do we guarantee a particular score?

We can’t guarantee a specific IELTS score at Sevenine, because success depends a lot on each student’s own effort. However, we regularly run IELTS mock tests for our students, and their results often match their real exam results. This helps students prepare better and understand what they still need to improve before the real test. We also have many students who have achieved high scores of 7 and above — you can see them on our Instagram page. These examples show how well our approach to teaching and IELTS preparation works.

Is the trial lesson free?

The trial lesson is free if the student doesn’t continue in that group. In that case, we find another option for the student (a different teacher or group, depending on what didn’t suit them). As soon as the student says they want to continue after the trial lesson, the trial lesson counts as their first of 12 lessons and is paid according to the tariff.

What is the difference between Academic IELTS and General Training IELTS?

Academic IELTS is for people who want to go into higher education or professional registration, while General Training IELTS is for migration, work or life experience. At our school we only offer Academic IELTS. Even so, students preparing for Academic IELTS can easily manage General Training, because it is easier. In General Training, the reading and writing sections are lighter: the reading texts are not academic articles but simpler texts from everyday life. In writing, the difference is in Task 1: in Academic IELTS, Task 1 is describing graphs, charts and other visuals, while in General Training you write a letter.

What is the difference between UKVI IELTS and regular IELTS?

UKVI IELTS (IELTS for UK Visas and Immigration) is a test recognised by UK Visas and Immigration for immigration and visa purposes. It assesses your listening, reading, writing and speaking skills in English. There are three UKVI IELTS formats: Academic, General Training and Life Skills. The main difference from regular IELTS is that UKVI IELTS often requires higher scores for visa and immigration applications, and its results are used specifically for entry to the UK. Students who complete the IELTS Academic course at our centre can take UKVI IELTS without any problem.

What is the difference between the paper and computer versions of IELTS?

The main difference between paper and computer IELTS is the format. In the paper version, reading, listening and writing are done on paper; in the computer version, on a computer. The speaking part can be face to face with an examiner or by video call (Zoom). Computer test results usually come in 3–5 days, paper test results in 13 days. Some candidates find it easier to do everything on paper, while others prefer typing on a computer because of typing speed and being able to delete letters.

Do we sign a contract with every student?

We don’t sign a contract with every student. Instead, we have a public offer.

A public offer is an offer of goods or services in advertising and other public materials that describes the terms of purchase. Unlike a regular offer, it is addressed to an unlimited number of people and invites them to enter into a contract.

At Sevenine, the public offer is accepted by attending lessons or using our bonuses, such as the Sevenine Shop, Speaking Workshops, the mobile app and so on. If a student decides to continue after the trial lesson, they accept the terms of the public offer in full. You can read our public offer at the reception desk in our centre, in the mobile app and in the student handbook.
